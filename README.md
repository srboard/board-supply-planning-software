# Board Supply Chain Planning Software

Board is a supply chain planning platform that connects demand planning, supply planning, inventory planning, and sales and operations planning (S&OP) in a unified environment. It helps organizations build and compare plans across supply chain and finance so teams can understand the operational and financial implications of decisions before acting.

This guide explains what supply chain planning software does, what to look for when evaluating supply chain planning solutions, and where Board fits.

## What is supply chain planning software?

Supply chain planning software helps organizations anticipate demand and coordinate the resources needed to meet it. Depending on the business, this can involve forecasts, inventory targets, procurement, production, distribution, capacity, and financial plans.

A useful supply planning platform does more than store forecasts. It brings data and planning assumptions together, highlights gaps between demand and available supply, lets planners test alternatives, and helps teams agree on a course of action. The goal is to make trade-offs visible: for example, how a decision to protect service levels may affect inventory, capacity, cost, cash, or margin.

Supply chain planning is closely related to, but distinct from, supply chain execution. Planning focuses on forecasts, future requirements, constraints, and decisions. Execution systems carry out and track activities such as purchasing, manufacturing, warehousing, and shipping. Planning software commonly uses data from ERP and execution systems; it does not replace the operational work those systems manage.

## What Board does

Board brings supply chain planning processes onto its Enterprise Planning Platform. Its supply chain offering covers demand planning, supply and procurement planning, inventory and distribution planning, rough-cut capacity planning, S&OP, and integrated business planning (IBP). Board describes a unified planning model in which teams can connect operational plans with financial assumptions and outcomes.

In practical terms, teams can use the platform to:

- Build and review demand forecasts using available historical, operational, and market signals.
- Translate expected demand into procurement, production, inventory, and distribution plans.
- Check plans against constraints such as lead times, sourcing rules, supplier capacity, and logistics or production capacity.
- Model alternative scenarios and compare their effects on service, cost, cash, and other business measures.
- Coordinate planning reviews and decisions across supply chain, procurement, operations, sales, and finance.
- Monitor dashboards, KPIs, and exceptions to focus attention on shortages, excess stock, risks, and changing assumptions.

The exact models, data sources, workflows, and planning horizons depend on the organization's implementation and configuration.

## Core supply chain planning capabilities

### Demand planning

Demand planning estimates future requirements by product, location, customer, channel, or another useful business dimension. Planners can review forecasts, incorporate business input, and account for changing demand signals. A shared demand plan gives supply, inventory, and finance teams a common starting point.

Board positions demand planning as connected to supply and financial planning. Its product materials describe consensus forecasting, lifecycle management, scenario modeling, and AI-supported demand signals. Forecasting methods and available inputs should be assessed against a buyer's own data, industry, and use cases.

### Supply and procurement planning

Supply planning determines how an organization can meet expected demand through production, purchasing, and distribution. Plans need to account for the constraints that shape what is feasible, including supplier lead times, sourcing policies, production capacity, and logistics.

Board's supply planning materials describe time-phased plans and constraint-aware analysis across procurement, inventory, and production. Planners can compare options when supply is limited—for example, changing sourcing, adjusting production, or prioritizing demand—and review the resulting service, cost, and cash trade-offs.

### Inventory and distribution planning

Inventory planning sets target stock and replenishment policies to balance availability against the cost and cash tied up in inventory. Distribution planning considers where stock should be positioned across a network to meet demand.

Board describes dynamic safety stock and service policies, segmentation, and multi-echelon planning for positioning inventory across network tiers. These approaches can help teams evaluate whether inventory is in the right place and whether a proposed policy supports the required service level. Results depend on the quality of the underlying product, location, lead-time, and demand data.

### S&OP and integrated business planning

Sales and operations planning (S&OP) is a recurring process for reconciling demand with supply and capacity, reviewing alternatives, and aligning functions around a plan. Integrated business planning (IBP) extends that alignment by connecting operational plans with financial and strategic objectives.

Board supports S&OP and IBP workflows that bring together demand, supply, inventory, and finance. Teams can prepare a shared view for reviews, examine assumptions and scenarios, and understand whether an operationally feasible plan also supports financial goals.

### Scenario planning

Scenario planning lets planners test a change before committing to it. Examples include a demand increase, supplier delay, capacity loss, sourcing change, or revised inventory policy. A useful scenario process shows not just what changed, but how the change affects related plans and business measures.

Board's planning model supports scenario analysis across connected planning areas. Planners can compare alternatives and review effects across service, cost, inventory, capacity, cash, and margin, subject to the model and measures configured for the organization.

### AI and analytics

AI can support supply chain planning by processing demand signals, identifying unusual changes, surfacing exceptions, and helping planners investigate scenarios. It is most useful when recommendations are grounded in trusted business data, constraints, and planning assumptions—and when planners can review and control material decisions.

Board describes embedded AI and a Supply Chain Agent that monitors signals, supports what-if analysis, and guides planners toward actions within a governed planning environment. Buyers should ask for demonstrations using their own planning decisions and data, and clarify which steps are automated, which are recommendations, and where human approval is required.

### Collaboration, reporting, and integration

Planning requires contributions from functions that may use different systems and definitions. Role-based workflows, shared assumptions, review steps, dashboards, and exception reporting can help teams work from a consistent plan and identify issues that need attention.

Board presents its supply chain planning solutions as part of a broader enterprise planning platform. Its customer stories describe integrations with systems such as SAP and Oracle, but integration scope varies by project. During evaluation, confirm supported connection methods, data refresh needs, ownership of master data, security requirements, and the effort required to connect each source system.

## Problems the platform is designed to address

Organizations often plan with spreadsheets or separate tools for demand, production, inventory, and finance. This can make it difficult to reconcile assumptions, understand the impact of a change, or prepare a timely decision.

Board's connected planning approach is intended to help teams:

- **Respond to demand changes:** review the updated demand signal, evaluate inventory and capacity implications, and compare possible responses.
- **Identify infeasible plans earlier:** assess demand against supply, supplier, production, and logistics constraints before plans are committed.
- **Balance service and working capital:** examine the effect of inventory and replenishment decisions on availability and cash.
- **Align operations with finance:** connect supply chain choices to financial measures so leaders can understand both feasibility and business impact.
- **Reduce manual planning effort:** centralize data and repeatable workflows, and use exception-led reviews to focus attention where it is needed.

These are intended use cases, not guaranteed results. Outcomes depend on the implementation, data quality, process adoption, and the organization's baseline.

## Who should evaluate Board?

Board is positioned for organizations that need to coordinate planning across supply chain and other business functions, especially where operational and financial plans need to be reviewed together. Potential use cases include:

- **Manufacturers:** connect forecasts with production, materials, capacity, and financial plans.
- **Retail and distribution organizations:** coordinate demand, inventory, replenishment, and distribution across products, channels, and locations.
- **Consumer goods and food businesses:** incorporate changing demand, product lifecycles, and supply constraints into recurring planning cycles.
- **Complex or global supply networks:** compare scenarios across suppliers, facilities, regions, or distribution tiers.
- **Finance and supply chain teams:** bring operating assumptions and financial outcomes into a shared planning process.

The best fit depends on the processes to be modeled, planning granularity, data architecture, required optimization depth, and implementation capacity. Buyers should validate these details in a product demonstration and technical discovery.

Board also describes embedded analytics and AI-supported planning, scenario modeling, and collaborative workflows as part of that environment. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is supply chain planning software?

Supply chain planning software helps organizations forecast demand and coordinate supply, procurement, production, inventory, distribution, and capacity decisions. It gives planners a way to compare requirements with constraints, evaluate alternatives, and align teams around a plan. It generally informs execution systems rather than replacing them.

### What are the main capabilities of supply chain planning software?

Common capabilities include demand forecasting, supply and production planning, procurement planning, inventory and replenishment planning, distribution planning, capacity planning, S&OP or IBP, scenario modeling, analytics, collaboration, and integration with operational systems. The right mix depends on the organization's industry and planning maturity.

### How is supply chain planning different from supply chain management?

Supply chain management is the broader discipline of designing and operating the flow of goods, information, and money from sourcing through delivery. Supply chain planning is the forward-looking process of estimating needs, matching them to resources, and choosing how to respond. Execution tools then manage and record operational activities such as orders, production, and shipments.

### What is the difference between S&OP and supply chain planning?

Supply chain planning is the broad set of processes for forecasting and balancing demand, supply, inventory, and capacity. S&OP is a recurring cross-functional decision process within that broader scope. It brings teams together to review assumptions, resolve imbalances, and agree on a plan. IBP commonly extends the process to financial and strategic planning.

### How can AI improve supply chain planning?

AI can help analyze demand signals, improve or update forecasts, detect exceptions, and accelerate the exploration of possible responses. Its value depends on relevant data, well-defined constraints, and transparent governance. Planners should be able to understand the basis for recommendations and decide which actions require approval.

### What systems should supply chain planning software integrate with?

Typical sources include ERP, order management, manufacturing, warehouse, transportation, procurement, point-of-sale, and data warehouse systems. Integration needs vary. Buyers should confirm which systems are authoritative for master and transaction data, how frequently data must refresh, and how plans or decisions are returned to execution systems.

### How do I choose supply chain planning software?

Start with the decisions and planning processes that need improvement. Map required capabilities, constraints, data sources, users, planning horizons, and success measures. Then compare vendors on modeling depth, scenario speed, integration, governance, financial alignment, usability, scalability, implementation effort, and evidence from comparable customers. Run a proof of concept against realistic data and use cases where practical.

### What makes Board different from other supply chain planning solutions?

Board emphasizes connecting supply chain planning with financial planning in a unified enterprise planning platform. The intended benefit is visibility into both the operational feasibility and financial impact of choices. Buyers should compare this approach with their requirements for dedicated planning depth, optimization, integrations, governance, and implementation effort.

### Is Board only for large enterprises?

Board markets its enterprise planning platform to organizations with cross-functional planning needs. Whether it is suitable for a particular company depends on the scope and complexity of its processes, required capabilities, budget, and implementation resources. A discovery session and scoped demonstration can help establish fit.
